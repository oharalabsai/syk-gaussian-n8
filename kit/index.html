GIST hardware kit: wormhole-signal circuits that beat Eq. 11 in noisy simulation, in canonical term order (order-optimised SYM: tie at best)

In plain words: we searched sparse Gaussian SYK models (8 Majoranas, 24 terms) for ones whose traversable-wormhole signal (the mutual-information jump dI between the probe P and the readout T when the coupling sign flips) survives hardware noise better than the published Eq. 11 model. 2 listings, both from one term set (the recommended pick), keep more signal than Eq. 11 on both protocols (FIX and SYM) in a FakeNighthawk simulation on all three routing-seed lists, with both sides in canonical term order. 1 more listing, on a second term set, is borderline: it passes the pre-registered rule on seed list A (FIX +2.8%, SYM +0.8%) but falls below Eq. 11 on the held-out lists B and C. This folder holds the routed circuits ready to run, the same-pipeline Eq. 11 circuits as the positive control, and two typical random draws as the negative control.

Noise-scale stress (errors x0.5 / x1 / x2: 19277_201 beats Eq. 11 at every scale; 155_129 FIX falls to Eq. 11 near x1.55), the JW map (shipped S2; S3 variants in each S3/ folder) with the measured physical qubits, and the term overlap between options: README.md.

What to run

Each folder: FIX_mu-12.qasm, FIX_mu+12.qasm, SYM_mu-12.qasm, SYM_mu+12.qasm, run as is; two-qubit tomography of P and register qubit 1 (physical qubits in each header). Prediction to test: 19277_201 > 155_129 > Eq. 11 on both protocols, and noisy dI = dI0 × a e−b·2q (FIX a 1.088, b 0.00540; SYM a 1.209, b 0.00561). Shots and caveats: README.md. Everything: gist_kit.zip.

Caveats

FakeNighthawk is a proxy for the device. The win holds with both sides in the canonical term order; with order-optimised SYM it is a tie at best. The listings are rare selections (about 1 in 1.5e13), and so far come from one distinct term set.
